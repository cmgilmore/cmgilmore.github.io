FAQ

Questions people actually ask

About working together, EMDR, privacy, fees, and insurance.

Working together

Who do you work with?

I work with adults in Colorado in two main groups. The first is anyone living with the effects of trauma, whether that’s a single event, a difficult childhood, a painful relationship, or loss. You don’t need to be a professional or leader to work with me. The second is professionals and leaders (executives, founders, physicians, attorneys, and others in demanding roles), with or without a trauma history, who are dealing with burnout, high-functioning anxiety, perfectionism, or the sense that what built their success is now costing them.

Do I need to have “real” trauma to work with you?

No. Many of my clients, especially busy professionals, would never describe themselves as having trauma. They describe being on edge, unable to switch off, overreacting to feedback, or feeling numb at moments that should feel good. Sometimes those patterns trace back to a clear event; often they trace back to years of chronic pressure, an unpredictable childhood home, or learning early that being impressive was the safest way to belong. You don’t need a label to start. We can figure out together what’s driving things.

What happens in the first session?

We get oriented. I’ll ask about what brought you in, what you’ve already tried, your history, and what you’d want to be different in six months. You’ll also get a sense of how I work. Nothing intense happens in session one; trauma work only begins once there’s enough stability and trust to make it useful.

How long does therapy usually take?

It depends on what we’re working on. Focused work on a specific issue or memory can show meaningful change in a few months. Longer-standing patterns, especially ones rooted in childhood, usually take longer. We’ll set goals together and check in on progress regularly so you’re never guessing whether this is working.

Do you see clients in person or online?

Both. I see clients in person at my office in Wheat Ridge (4251 Kipling St #230) and online by secure video (telehealth) anywhere in Colorado. Many busy professionals mix the two. I also see a limited number of clients on Tuesdays and Thursdays in Evergreen through Insight Psychology; contact me directly for availability there.

Can I work with you if I live outside Colorado?

I can only provide therapy to people who are physically located in Colorado at the time of the session, including online sessions. If you travel frequently, let’s talk about how to schedule around it.

EMDR and approach

What is EMDR, in plain terms?

EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing) is a structured trauma therapy. While you briefly focus on a distressing memory, you also follow a back-and-forth (bilateral) stimulus, like eye movements or alternating taps. Over a series of sessions, the memory tends to lose its emotional charge: you still remember it, but it stops hijacking your body in the present. More on EMDR.

Is EMDR evidence-based?

Yes. EMDR is one of the most studied trauma therapies and is included as a recommended treatment for PTSD in major clinical practice guidelines, including those from the World Health Organization and the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs and Department of Defense. It is not a fit for every person or every problem, which is why we assess fit first.

Will I have to talk through every detail of what happened?

No. One reason EMDR works well for many people is that it does not require you to narrate traumatic events in detail. You stay in control of what you share.

Is this coaching?

No. Coaching focuses on performance and goals. Therapy addresses the underlying patterns, nervous system responses, and history that drive how you perform and how you feel. My leadership background means I understand the world professionals and leaders live in, but what I offer is clinical mental health care.

Privacy, fees, and insurance

How private is therapy with you?

What you share is confidential, with the standard legal exceptions (such as imminent risk of harm), which I review with you at the start. Because I am a private-pay practice, nothing is billed to your insurance unless you choose to submit a superbill yourself, so your employer-sponsored plan has no record of your sessions. If you work in a small industry or a visible role, or simply value privacy, we can talk about anything else that would help you feel comfortable.

How much does a session cost?

$165 per 50-minute session, in person or online. The first 15-minute consultation is free, and a limited number of sliding-scale spots are available. I’m a private-pay practice, so nothing is billed to your insurance or employer plan. If your plan includes out-of-network benefits, I can provide a superbill you can submit for possible reimbursement. Therapy is often an eligible HSA or FSA expense.

You have the right to a Good Faith Estimate of expected costs. Ask me anytime.

Do you take insurance?

I don’t bill insurance directly. I can provide a superbill you can submit for possible out-of-network reimbursement; check your plan’s out-of-network mental health benefits first.

What does LPCC mean, and why does supervision matter?

LPCC stands for Licensed Professional Counselor Candidate, Colorado’s license for counselors completing their supervised post-graduate hours toward full LPC licensure. I meet regularly with two experienced supervisors, a licensed clinical psychologist who is an EMDRIA-approved consultant and a Licensed Professional Counselor, so my work with you has more experienced eyes behind it. About my supervisors.

What if I’m in crisis?

I’m not able to provide emergency services. If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), call Colorado Crisis Services at 1-844-493-8255, or call 911.
