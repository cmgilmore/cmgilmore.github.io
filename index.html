What connects them

A nervous system that learned to stay on guard

These two groups have more in common than it might seem. Whether it came from one frightening event, years of an unpredictable home, or a decade of nonstop pressure, the result is often the same: a nervous system that learned staying on alert was the safest option, and kept doing it long after the danger passed.

For some people that looks like panic, avoidance, or nightmares. For others it looks like overworking, perfectionism, and never quite being able to relax. Same root, different expressions. The work in both cases is helping your system learn that it’s safe to stand down.

How I work

Not just insight. A different felt sense.

Many people already understand their patterns. The work here is about changing how safe and settled you actually feel, day to day.

EMDR

A structured, well-researched trauma therapy that helps distressing memories lose their emotional charge, without having to retell every detail. About EMDR

Nervous system work

Drawing on polyvagal theory to recognize when your body is in fight, flight, or shutdown, and to build real capacity for calm and connection.

Attachment-based

Understanding how early relationships shaped the way you connect, work, and handle conflict today, and building more secure ways of relating.

Direct and collaborative

You’re the expert on your life. I bring structure, honesty, and the occasional joke. Growth doesn’t have to be humorless.

About

Clinical trauma training, and twenty years of knowing what pressure feels like

Before becoming a therapist, I spent close to two decades in executive leadership in higher education. I watched talented people quietly burn out, and I knew the gap between how things look and how they feel from the inside, including, honestly, in my own mirror some mornings.

That’s what pulled me into clinical counseling. Today my work centers on trauma, using EMDR and attachment-based, nervous-system-informed approaches, under the supervision of an EMDRIA Approved Consultant. Whether you’re here because of something that happened or because of how hard you’ve been running, you won’t have to perform in this room.
